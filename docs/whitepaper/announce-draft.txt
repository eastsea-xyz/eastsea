Subject: EastSea: a public network experiment on Macs, for critique

I've been working on EastSea, an experiment around one question:
Can ordinary Macs run and prove a public network with no owner?

The paper describes a drawn Simplex BFT committee, threshold BLS
certificates, asynchronous Jolt/Akita execution proofs on Metal, certified
snapshot sync, and prunable history. It records testnet evidence and
distinguishes implemented rules from proposals. [1]

In a regional-link simulation with 20 Mbps upload per validator and
approximately 50 KB payloads, 16 seats had empirical p99 finality of
0.699002 seconds. [2] This is simulation evidence, not a distributed-Mac
measurement. A recorded M1 Max/Metal warm sample proved 50 transactions
in 47.2 seconds. [1]

This is early and unproven, without an independent security audit.
Proofs can lag or be absent; admission and software updates retain
operating keys. Please do not participate expecting money. [1]

I'd welcome critique of the consensus and proving assumptions, code and
paper review, reports of what breaks, and measurements from your own Mac.
