Subject: Can the Mac on your desk help keep a public network alive?

Fifty transactions took 47.2 seconds to prove on an M1 Max.
The network doesn't wait for the proof. Is that a trade you would accept? [1]

I started EastSea to test a question: can ordinary Macs run and prove a
public network with no owner? Picture the Mac on your desk checking the
network itself. The difficult part is whether enough Macs can keep it
available, admit newcomers, and prove the work. [2]

The paper lays out what we've tried: committee finality, Metal-enabled
Jolt/Akita proofs, certified snapshots, and prunable history. It separates
testnet evidence from planned rules. Proofs can lag or be absent. Admission
and software updates still have operating keys. This is early, unproven,
and not independently audited. Please don't join expecting money. [2]

Our regional-link simulation, with 20 Mbps upload and roughly 50 KB payloads,
recorded 16-seat p99 finality of 0.699002 seconds. Physical-Mac results remain
to be measured. [2]

Run a node and tell us what breaks. Challenge the consensus and proving
assumptions, review the code and paper, or send measurements from your Mac.
