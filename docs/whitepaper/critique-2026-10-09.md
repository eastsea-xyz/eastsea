# EastSea: the hardest questions before sending

**Review of the 2026-10-09 draft; completed 2026-10-10 KST. Documents only. Nothing posted.**

The strongest objections concern present control, the validity that clients
actually verify, historical signing shares, and recovery when a quorum is lost.
The paper already admits many of these limits. Repeating an admitted limit is
not discovering a hidden vulnerability; it can still identify evidence needed
before the central experiment is credible. The most concrete text errors are
the inclusive committee ceiling, verifier-outage behavior, and key-role and
revocation wording. The emails need the same boundaries as the paper.

This review covers [the English paper](eastsea.md), [its Korean translation](eastsea.ko.md),
[plain](announce-plain.txt), [requested draft](announce-draft.txt), and
[hook](announce-hook.txt) emails, [source notes](source-notes.md),
[community research](../research/community-2026-10-09.md), and relevant cited
implementation and research sources. Reviewed checkout:
`819694c874929c792ca0ca4022d7a17f597ab7aa`, branch `codex/critic`.
The paper's implementation baseline is
`345c36a4999cb3cb9a28e5f23947ef4c2a040cf8`; both commits are dated 2026-10-09.
Their diff changes documents/legal copy, not implementation. Cross-branch
sources below were read through their immutable git objects without changing
another lane's checkout. No builds, nodes, guest compilation, new measurements,
remote-host work, or release qualification were performed.

Ranks are editorial: potential to invalidate the security/trust model comes
first, then operational feasibility and economic framing, then narrower
misreadings. There are six questions in each reader's voice. The quoted paper
and email text is from the checkout **before** the separate correction commit;
section labels remain useful after line numbers change. Suggested wording is
an instruction for that commit, not evidence that an open problem was solved.

## The thirty questions, ranked by damage if unanswered

### 1. Cypherpunk — “Who can stop it today? Put the founder's actual quorum ahead of ‘no owner’.”

**Answer.** The ownership question remains unresolved. Up to three founder
reserve keys on one Mac can occupy a four-seat committee and satisfy its
three-share quorum (P1). They can then authorize certificates without another
controller or halt progress by withholding. Reserves can return under the
availability policy; independent controller diversity is not established by
address counts. This is an allowed configuration, not a measurement of the
current live roster. [rotation.rs, `with_reserve`](../../crates/node/src/rotation.rs)
and [reserve recovery](../ops/reserve-keys.md) support the answer (S0).

**Classification:** valid criticism of framing; interpreting the paper's
question as an achieved property is a misunderstanding.

**Exact text:** Abstract: “Can ordinary Macs run and prove a public network
with no owner?”; emails repeat it. Keep the research question, but identify
voting admission as gated in the abstract and immediately disclose founder
bootstrap-quorum power in the emails. In §1 add: “Public reading and source
access coexist with permissioned voting admission. Founder reserve keys can
hold a bootstrap quorum; independent operation is an objective, not a present
guarantee.”

### 2. ZK researcher — “Where does my client check the argument? I found a verifier returning `true`.”

**Answer.** New uncertified proof claims reach the real sidecar through
[`chain::pay_proofs`](../../crates/node/src/chain.rs) and
[`program::verify`](../../apps/prover/src/program.rs), which verifies
the argument against the expected public output and no-panic result. Certified
replay substitutes `Certified::verify`, returning `true`; followers install
that certificate-trusting verifier. Wallet block-proof verification is absent
([proving design](../design/06-proving.md), S0). Thus proofs really are checked
on validator admission, but these clients cannot independently detect a
committee certifying invalid proof acceptance. M2's verification timings concern
the sidecar, not the wallet path.

**Classification:** valid criticism of client assurance; “no proof is ever
checked” is a misunderstanding.

**Exact text:** §8: “today's followers and wallets mostly rely on committee
certification.” Replace with: “Followers and wallets currently trust committee
certification of proof acceptance; they do not cryptographically verify block
proofs themselves.” Explain certified replay's deliberate trust shortcut in §4.

### 3. Bitcoin developer — “Why does the fault bound stop at today's committee when yesterday's shares still sign under the same key?”

**Answer.** It cannot stop there. A usable quorum retained from **one former
sharing** can still sign under the stable group identity. Shares from different
resharings are not simply additive. [Light verification](../../crates/light/src/lib.rs)
and [epoch verification](../../crates/node/src/epochs.rs) check that identity,
not the actual signer roster. [Supervisor retirement](../../crates/node/src/supervisor.rs)
replaces/removes active shares and attempts old-generation cleanup. Its erase
path uses [atomic replacement](../../crates/node/src/atomic.rs), which does not
establish destruction of old inode contents, SSD/APFS copies or backups.
The [reserve runbook](../ops/reserve-keys.md) requires share backups. This is
static threat-model analysis (S0), not a reproduced alternate-history attack.

**Classification:** valid criticism. Resharing is not cryptographic revocation.

**Exact text:** §8: “Protecting and retiring old shares is therefore part of
the threat model”. State the stronger assumption explicitly: “Safety also
assumes that an adversary cannot obtain a usable quorum from any former
sharing. File replacement and cleanup do not establish destruction of backup
or snapshot copies.”

### 4. Security auditor — “Who can replace the binary that requests my signatures, and what authenticates its first installation?”

**Answer.** The configured new-genesis update gate checks certified ReleaseLog
state/code, builder signatures and archive hash; Sparkle adds distribution
authentication (P4). Legacy testnet uses Sparkle alone. The first installed app
supplies the pins in its own bundled configuration. The running gate cannot
prove its own initial authenticity. Sufficient approved signers can distribute
replacement wallet/node code and future trust rules. If certified network
state is unavailable, automatic approval can also be unavailable. Distinct keys
do not establish distinct controllers. See [ReleaseApproval.swift](../../apps/wallet/Sources/ReleaseApproval.swift)
and [ReleaseUpdateGate.swift](../../apps/wallet/Sources/ReleaseUpdateGate.swift)
(S0); no production installation result is supplied (M7).

**Classification:** valid criticism. A lone publisher bypassing correctly
configured builder checks is a misunderstanding.

**Exact text:** §7.3: “The app verifies log state and code, archive hash, and
the release signatures.” Add: “The pins come from the installed app, so first
installation remains a distribution trust assumption. Without usable certified
network state, the automatic update gate defers installation; an emergency
manual replacement needs independent artifact authentication.”

### 5. Cypherpunk — “If Pipln disappears or Apple refuses service, what participation right survives?”

**Answer.** Reading/source access does not imply an unconditional voting right.
The registrar authorizes admission and reattestation after off-chain Apple
checks ([devicecheck.rs](../../crates/node/src/devicecheck.rs)). After applicable
coverage/grace, missing reattestation prevents required accepted liveness
answers ([beacons.rs](../../crates/rewards/src/beacons.rs)) and affects future
draw eligibility ([rotation.rs](../../crates/node/src/rotation.rs)). It does
not erase prior registrations or directly disable current votes. The
[proof-issuance registration check](../../crates/rewards/src/lib.rs) is separate,
so previously registered operators need not lose proof issuance merely because
fresh reattestation is unavailable. Registrar replacement needs a functioning
committee; no sunset is promised. These are S0 rules, not an outage experiment.

**Classification:** valid criticism; “the registrar alone instantly pauses
finality or seizes balances” is a misunderstanding.

**Exact text:** §7.3: “Apple or registrar refusal can affect existing candidates
after their reattestation grace, not just new entrants.” Replace “affect” with
the concrete beacon/future-eligibility effects and preserve the current-vote
and registration distinctions above.

### 6. Security auditor — “Which keys can actually be copied? Do not let the wallet's Secure Enclave stand in for consensus protection.”

**Answer.** [Wallet keys](../../apps/wallet/Sources/EnclaveKey.swift) and
[agent keys](../../apps/agent/Sources/Keys.swift) use Secure Enclave handles.
Consensus differs: [`validator.key`](../../crates/node/src/roster.rs) serializes
Ed25519 consensus and iroh seeds; [`threshold.json`](../../crates/node/src/dkg.rs)
holds the BLS share. Owner-only permissions protect exportable secrets.
[`key_binding.rs`](../../crates/node/src/key_binding.rs) supplies a local
hardware-identifier guard and deliberate owner recovery, not nonexportability
or on-chain evidence that each vote came from the checked Mac. Source inventory
only (S0); production extraction/host-isolation assurance is absent.

**Classification:** valid criticism of an omitted role boundary; treating the
agent-account paragraph as an explicit promise about every node key is a
misunderstanding.

**Exact text:** §6: “Agent accounts use a device-local Secure Enclave key with
an owner-authorized session.” Retain and add: “Consensus identity seeds and
BLS shares instead live in owner-only files and are exportable. The Mac-binding
guard is a local software control, not a nonexportable consensus key or an
on-chain hardware proof.”

### 7. Bitcoin developer — “What survives journal loss or rollback, and who authorizes recovery after the old quorum is gone?”

**Answer.** [Inline/DurableVote integration](../../crates/node/src/engine.rs)
and [voting.rs](../../crates/node/src/voting.rs) gate honest votes on execution
and durable storage. Journal/epoch guards refuse voting when local evidence
indicates prior voting with lost safety state. They do not establish a
hardware-backed monotonic record detecting restoration of a complete, old,
internally consistent key/journal/marker backup. Refusal reduces online seats.
Fresh [handoff](../../crates/node/src/handoff.rs) and
[DKG](../../crates/node/src/dkg.rs) still require the old quorum. Permanent loss
has no automatic repair under the existing anchor. M5's stalls and the
[recovery runbook](../ops/consensus-recovery.md) are adverse operational
evidence, not exhaustive power-loss or rollback qualification.

**Classification:** valid criticism. The BFT model does not promise safety and
availability after arbitrary durable-state loss.

**Exact text:** §3.1: “Votes must also follow the application rules and preserve
their safety journals across restarts.” Add that voting history must resist
rollback, the guards do not establish complete-backup rollback detection, and
recovery cannot replace an unobtainable old quorum.

### 8. ZK researcher — “Write the relation. Which state transition do I actually learn was valid?”

**Answer.** For the accepted guest, the argument establishes that some advice
causes no-panic execution and output `claim(C, prover)`. [`BlockStatement`](../../crates/proving/src/block.rs)
commits context, transaction hashes, activations, pre/post roots and gas, with
payout address bound by the claim. The guest reconstructs the witness and
executes transactions. The node's pre-root is **after system writes**; its
current statement supplies empty activations ([chain.rs](../../crates/node/src/chain.rs),
`statement`). Issuance, earlier proof payouts, registrar/roster changes,
receipts and ancestry from genesis remain outside this relation. A valid
transaction argument cannot repair an invalid preceding system transition.
This is S0 source analysis; M2 shows sample generation, not whole-chain validity.

**Classification:** valid criticism of the assurance scope; the paper already
discloses the main exclusion.

**Exact text:** §4: “Current block proofs therefore do not establish the
correctness of reward issuance, proof payouts, registrar rotation, or committee
changes.” **No correction required.** Keep it and add: “This is a
transaction-execution argument from an authenticated intermediate state, not
a proof of full-chain validity from genesis.”

### 9. ZK researcher — “What soundness analysis covers these exact forks, parameters and Metal kernels?”

**Answer.** [`jolt-fork.lock`](../../scripts/jolt-fork.lock) identifies the Jolt
and Akita revisions and archive hashes. [Backend features](../../apps/prover/Cargo.toml)
select CPU or Metal Akita. Seed-derived matrix/prefix comparisons described in
the M2 report check setup consistency; sidecar self-tests check selected
positive and negative vectors ([engine.rs](../../apps/prover/src/engine.rs)).
Neither establishes cryptographic soundness, transcript/parameter correctness,
inline constraints, or Metal-kernel equivalence. No independently reviewed,
deployment-specific numerical soundness bound is established by the cited
sources. Release program pinning identifies a program; it is not a theorem or
an attestation of the sidecar executable. [Audit scope](../research/audit-7-2026-10-06.md)
excludes these assurances (M7).

**Classification:** valid criticism. Do not manufacture a security-bit figure
or borrow an upstream theorem for an unaudited fork.

**Exact text:** §4: “Public setup matrices are seed-derived, with verifier
prefixes embedded and checked by repository tests.” Rewrite to attribute
those checks to fork setup consistency and add that independent audit and a
deployment-specific soundness analysis remain absent.

### 10. Bitcoin developer — “What prevents approved software with the same version from disagreeing, or an upgrade from losing quorum?”

**Answer.** [`upgrade.rs`](../../crates/node/src/upgrade.rs) authenticates the
schedule; [`chain.rs`](../../crates/node/src/chain.rs), `admissible_upgrade` and
`next_schedule`, enforce notice and declared version. Unsupported binaries
refuse the activated protocol. P4 describes actual notice/approval rules.
They coordinate declarations, not software semantics. A same-version bug can
still create disagreement; too few compatible online voters can halt at
activation. Release and protocol approvals have different authorities. The
[launch runbook](../ops/mainnet-launch.md) requests replay/rehearsal; M9 proves
one historical test transition, not universal split safety. An operator can
refuse installation without retaining compatibility on the same network.

**Classification:** valid criticism; authenticated activation does not prevent
every consensus split.

**Exact text:** §7.3: “changing those rules requires the committee upgrade path
and matching software.” Add that declared unsupported versions refuse,
same-version semantic disagreement remains possible, and insufficiently
coordinated installation can remove the quorum.

### 11. Bitcoin developer — “Does ‘sixteen-seat ceiling’ include the founder reserves added after the capped draw?”

**Answer.** Not as an invariant established by this source. Ordinary
[`draw_spread_capped`](../../crates/node/src/rotation.rs) takes the configured
cap. [`chain.rs`](../../crates/node/src/chain.rs), draw and epoch-boundary paths,
subsequently apply `with_reserve`, which takes no cap and appends reserve
members. [`rewards::commit_roster`](../../crates/rewards/src/lib.rs) stores the
result without that size check; [handoff verification](../../crates/node/src/handoff.rs)
checks membership/readiness, not this configured cap. Consequently P1's
configuration is not proof of an inclusive active-roster maximum. M1 measures
the stated tiers, not every reserve-augmented roster. This is a static gap,
not a measured oversize live roster.

**Classification:** valid criticism and a concrete overstatement.

**Exact text:** §3.3: “This paper adopts **16 as the proposed launch ceiling**”.
Replace with “**16 active seats as the proposed launch performance target**”,
explain the ordinary/genesis cap versus reserve override, and require inclusive
enforcement or a measured, published exception policy before calling it a
ceiling. Update the §9.1 interpretation and §10 table consistently.

### 12. ZK researcher — “When your verifier dies, does only one proposal fail, or does the voting process exit?”

**Answer.** An invalid proof rejects the proposal. Persistent `Unavailable`
in [`Verifier::decide`](../../crates/node/src/prover.rs) exits `EXIT_FATAL_TASK`
for supervised recovery. [`install_verifier`](../../crates/node/src/main.rs)
also stops protocol-2 voters without a functioning verifier. Affected voters
can therefore disappear/restart and reduce the online quorum. Finality still
does not require generating an execution proof for every earlier block.
Slow proving and unavailable **verification** are different faults. No recorded
candidate-specific outage/recovery measurement is supplied (S0, M7).

**Classification:** valid criticism and a concrete correction.

**Exact text:** §4: “Verification failure or an unavailable verification sidecar
prevents that validator from accepting the proof-bearing proposal. It does not
make all other blocks wait for proving.” Replace with: “An invalid proof makes
the proposal unacceptable. A persistent verifier outage stops the affected
validator for supervised recovery, which can reduce the available quorum.
Finality does not require generating proofs for every earlier block.”

### 13. Cypherpunk — “Your code calls distinct addresses ‘independent’. Where is independent control proved?”

**Answer.** It is not. [Reward aggregation](../../crates/rewards/src/lib.rs)
takes the largest Mac weight per address. [Ordinary seating and `independent`](../../crates/node/src/rotation.rs)
count registration addresses. One controller can own multiple admitted Macs
and addresses. An honest DeviceCheck registrar introduces a device admission
cost, not a proof of personhood, common-control exclusion or organizational
diversity. Registrar compromise further weakens that admission premise.
P2's issuance cap and P1's seat rules do not change the controller fault model.
No operator-independence census is published (S0).

**Classification:** valid criticism of the model; a hidden per-human cap is a
misunderstanding.

**Exact text:** §7.1: “There is no claim that the one-sixteenth rule limits a
human's total rewards or voting power.” **No change required.** Preserve it.
In §7.2 qualify “four independent operators”: the implementation counts distinct
registration addresses and does not establish independent human control.

### 14. Bitcoin developer — “Is the draw fair, or just deterministic? Can an outgoing committee delay succession or exclude me?”

**Answer.** [`handoff.rs`](../../crates/node/src/handoff.rs), seed-message and
`accept_seed` paths, bind chain/draw in a separate domain. Pool freezing and
deterministic roster checks occur in [chain.rs](../../crates/node/src/chain.rs).
A unique threshold signature fixes the output for the fixed message; it does
not guarantee publication or secrecy from a threshold coalition. Under the
intended bound, enough honest online shares can still progress; withholding
does not automatically succeed with only `f` faulty seats. Availability ranking
precedes ticket tie-breaking. The readiness-subset exception checks retained
members but does not prove omitted members were unavailable. P1 supplies the
rules; no published fairness/exclusion campaign establishes the stronger claim.

**Classification:** valid criticism; an arbitrary registrar-chosen seed is a
misunderstanding.

**Exact text:** §3.2: “Thus the implemented selection is not a uniform lottery
over people.” Retain; add the fixed-output/timely-publication distinction and
committee-cooperation requirement. Preserve §3.4's existing omission warning.

### 15. Cypherpunk — “After everyone prunes, who supplies inconvenient old blocks? A commitment cannot recreate missing bytes.”

**Answer.** Correct. [Era retrieval](../../crates/node/src/era_net.rs) verifies
returned data, but explicitly fails when its upstream keeps no era. Baseline
[pruning](../../crates/node/src/prune.rs) can retain era files; the storage-defaults
branch recommends dropping them. P5's archive rewards are default-off with
unfinished activation gates; sampled retrieval does not establish independent
full replicas. M3 proves an empty-history local retention fixture, not resilient
distributed retention. There is no published recovery result after independent
holders disappear. Commitments detect tampering, not permanent loss.

**Classification:** valid criticism; authenticated history is not guaranteed
available history.

**Exact text:** §5: “Archive nodes retain the replay path.” Replace with:
“Archive nodes can retain the replay path. Replay from genesis remains possible
only while the required historical bytes are available from local storage or
reachable holders; no published retention result establishes that guarantee.”

### 16. HN reader — “What job needs another L1? Why wouldn't an existing chain or signed service do?”

**Answer.** The evidence establishes technical capabilities, not necessity or
external demand. [Contract measurements](../research/contracts-onchain-2026-10-06.md),
